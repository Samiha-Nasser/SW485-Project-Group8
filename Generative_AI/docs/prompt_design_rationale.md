The Thought Process Behind Each Template:

The Fraud Explanation Template (T1) was designed to transform the output of fraud detection models from a binary classification (Fraud / Not Fraud) into a human-understandable explanation. The design reflects the need for interpretability by linking the model’s prediction to the input transaction features, allowing the decision to be expressed in a simple textual form. This approach ensures that model outputs are not presented as opaque results, but rather as interpretable explanations grounded in the characteristics of the transaction.

The User Advice Template (T2) was developed based on the limitation that detection outputs alone are not sufficient for practical use. In real-world fraud systems, identifying suspicious activity must be followed by immediate and clear response actions. Therefore, this template translates the model’s prediction into direct security instructions, such as protecting the account or mitigating potential risk. This design reflects applied knowledge from fraud detection systems where outputs are operationalized into user-level responses.

The Fraud Risk Scoring Template (T3) was created to address the limitation of binary outputs by introducing a graded representation of risk. In fraud management, risk is typically not absolute but varies in severity depending on contextual features. Accordingly, this template integrates model predictions with transaction characteristics to produce a simplified risk level (Low, Medium, High), enabling clearer interpretation of the severity of each case.

The Comparative Fraud Analysis Template (T4) is based on the principle that fraudulent behavior often follows recurring and recognizable patterns. These patterns can be observed through transaction attributes such as timing, amount, and behavioral deviations. This template therefore compares the current transaction with known fraud patterns to highlight similarities that support the model’s prediction, providing a more contextual and structured explanation of why a transaction is considered suspicious.

 How domain knowledge influenced design:
 
 The design of the templates is grounded in established concepts used in modern fraud detection systems. The literature indicates that these systems do not rely solely on predictive accuracy, but also require explainability as a fundamental component in high-risk environments. Explainability plays a critical role in enabling fraud analysts and regulatory bodies to understand and justify model decisions, thereby enhancing system trustworthiness and compliance. Furthermore, prior studies highlight that fraud typically manifests through anomalous behavioral patterns that deviate from normal user behavior. This supports the inclusion of both an explanation template and a fraud-pattern comparison template, enabling the analysis of each transaction within its behavioral context rather than relying solely on the final classification outcome.

Building on this, the template design is extended to support multiple levels of decision-making. This includes a risk level estimation template, which translates model outputs into a graded representation of risk (e.g., low, medium, high) instead of a binary decision, aligning with real-world risk management practices in financial systems. In addition, a security action advice template is incorporated to convert analytical outputs into actionable recommendations, such as requesting additional verification or escalating cases for human review. This ensures that the system output is not limited to prediction and explanation, but is also operationally useful in practical fraud detection workflows.
